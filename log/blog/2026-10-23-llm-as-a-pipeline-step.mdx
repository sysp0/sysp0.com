My first LLM experiments felt like magic. Putting them into real pipelines felt like data engineering. Once an LLM call sits inside a pipeline that runs every 30 minutes, it has the same problems as any other step.

{/* truncate */}

## The questions I ask now

- **State.** What has already been processed? A rerun shouldn't pay to enrich the same ticket twice.
- **Retries.** What happens when a call fails or times out, and how many times do we try?
- **Rate limits.** How many calls can we make, and what happens when we hit the ceiling?
- **Cost.** What does each stage cost, and does it need the strongest model?
- **Tracing.** Can I see exactly what went in and what came out for any single result? This is where Langfuse earns its place.

## Where it showed up

In the support ticket pipeline, enrichment happens in layers: category, priority, resolution, agent quality. In the hotel review pipeline, each review is read first and each hotel is summarized second. In both, the model is one step among many.

## What I took from it

The hard part isn't getting an LLM to answer. It's making the answer repeatable, affordable and explainable.
